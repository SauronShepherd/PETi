Welcome to PETi, a safety-first AI assistant for pet care. This video follows a complete multi-agent workflow, from the owner's experience to the administrative view.

We begin in the user application. PETi helps owners understand visible evidence and organize follow-up care. It does not diagnose, prescribe treatment, or replace a veterinarian.

In the Agents workspace, the owner starts a review. The request enters a bounded workflow with explicit evidence and safety controls. The public preview is clearly labelled synthetic demo data.

We now open the administrative Agent Laboratory. This is the operational view of the multi-agent system. It shows the available agents, configured models, and execution history.

An orchestrator coordinates specialized stages. Evidence Intake checks that submitted evidence is usable and grounded. The Pet Specialist reviews permitted visible observations. Safety Review evaluates the result against deterministic PETi policy.

Agents may reason about evidence, but PETi remains authoritative. They cannot change ownership, access arbitrary data, choose unrestricted tools, or execute care actions independently.

Model Intelligence shows the configured model and safe execution metadata. Private prompts, tokens, hidden reasoning, and sensitive customer data are not exposed.

Live Runs gives every workflow a durable identifier and an explicit lifecycle. PETi records stages, status, evidence counts, claims, safety state, and operational metadata in the backend.

The run inspector shows how PETi reached its result without exposing chain of thought. Instead, reviewers see structured provenance: evidence used, claims produced, tools involved, and the safety decision.

Evidence and Claims separates observations from interpretations and keeps source references attached. Models and Tools shows bounded capabilities. Versions makes the runtime and policy configuration auditable.

A visible observation is not a diagnosis. A model suggestion is not an authoritative fact. And a proposed action is not an executed action.

On the user side, reminders require explicit approval. PETi binds approval to the exact proposed payload and persists an ActionReceipt only after the canonical care mutation succeeds.

This is PETi's bounded multi-agent approach: real orchestration, deterministic safety, least-privilege context, provenance, and human approval before action.
